1. What is a "Seed"? (The Analogy)
Imagine you are playing a game like Minecraft. When you create a new world, the game asks you for a "Seed."
If you leave it blank, you get a totally random world.
If you type "MountainView" (or any specific word/number), the game creates a specific world.
Crucially: If your friend types "MountainView" on their computer, they get the exact same world as you.
In programming, a "Seed" is just a number that forces the computer to make the "random" choices in the exact same order every time.

2. Why do we need it?
Computers cannot actually be random. They are logical machines. When you ask for a "random number," the computer is actually reading from a massive, pre-written list of scrambled numbers.
No Seed: The computer picks a starting spot on that list based on the current time (which changes every millisecond).
With Seed: You tell the computer: "Start reading the list at line 42." Since the list never changes, the numbers you get will always be the same.
